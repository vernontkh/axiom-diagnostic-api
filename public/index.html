Four-axiom self-audit

Everyone can state a principle.
Fewer can state where it breaks.

Four columns, one for each axiom your decisions rest on. For each, name your default, say how much weight it carries, and then name the condition that would make you abandon it.

The audit reads the second answer against the first. That distance — between what you claim and where you would fold — is the whole finding.

    Nothing is stored. The audit runs once, returns a reading, and keeps no record of your answers.

    On reading the result

    This describes one set of answers given on one afternoon. It is not a type, and you do not have one.

    Its only legitimate use is the next question it makes you ask — of yourself, or aloud with someone whose answers differ from yours. The moment a label becomes the reason you stopped examining something, it has done the opposite of its job. Discard it and answer again.
